Item 1. Business

Armada Freight Services is a privately held, asset-light carrier and third-party logistics provider focused on regional dry van truckload, dedicated contract carriage and LTL consolidation in the Southeast and South Central United States.

Armada operates 1,150 company tractors and 3,400 dry van trailers, and supplements its own fleet with a vetted network of about 2,800 partner carriers for overflow and surge capacity.

The Dallas to Atlanta corridor is Armada's highest-density lane, and the company runs a drop-trailer pool of 260 trailers at shipper facilities on that corridor.

Armada offers three service lines: regional truckload, dedicated fleets for retail and consumer packaged goods shippers, and an LTL consolidation program that builds multi-stop truckloads from LTL freight.

Armada's customer base is concentrated: its ten largest shippers generated 58% of revenue in 2025, and its largest shipper generated 14% of revenue.

Armada commits to a tender acceptance target of 92% on contracted lanes and publishes a weekly scorecard to each contract shipper.

The company employs about 1,600 people, including 1,240 professional drivers. Driver turnover was 64% in 2025, below the large truckload industry average reported by trade groups.

Armada's transportation management system provides electronic tendering through EDI and API connections, real-time tracking from electronic logging devices on every company tractor, and automated appointment scheduling.

Item 1A. Risk Factors

Armada's capacity during peak season is limited by its fleet size. In the fourth quarter of 2025 Armada relied on partner carriers for 31% of its loads, compared with 18% in the first three quarters, and its tender acceptance fell on surge volume.

Armada is privately held and its financial statements are not audited by a registered public accounting firm or filed with the SEC, so shippers cannot verify its financial condition from public filings.

Armada has a revolving credit facility of $60 million, of which $41 million was drawn at year-end 2025, which may limit its ability to add tractors if freight demand rises quickly.

Customer concentration is a risk: the loss of one of its ten largest shippers could reduce revenue and network density on its core lanes.

A shortage of qualified drivers could increase driver pay and recruiting costs and reduce the number of tractors Armada can keep seated.

Armada's insurance program uses a self-insured retention of $1 million per occurrence for auto liability, so a large accident claim could materially affect its results.

Fuel price volatility is passed through to shippers with a fuel surcharge schedule, but surcharges lag fuel price changes by about one week.
